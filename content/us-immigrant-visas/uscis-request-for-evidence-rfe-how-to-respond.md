## What an RFE Means

A Request for Evidence is a notice from USCIS stating that your application is missing required evidence or that the evidence provided does not yet prove eligibility. It pauses your case. It does not end it. Many approved applications went through an RFE first.

Common triggers include missing documents, unclear relationship or employment evidence, inconsistent dates, and eligibility questions that the original filing did not fully answer.

## RFE vs. NOID vs. Denial

* **RFE:** USCIS needs more evidence before deciding.
* **Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID):** USCIS has found a reason to deny and gives you a chance to respond. It is more serious than an RFE.
* **Denial:** USCIS has decided the case. Appeal or motion options may exist, with strict deadlines.

Treat every one of these notices as urgent and read it carefully.

## Step-by-Step: How to Respond to an RFE

### 1. Check the Deadline Immediately

The response deadline is printed on the notice. Count from the date on the notice, not the date you received it. USCIS does not grant extensions, and a late response is treated as abandonment of the request, which can lead to denial.

### 2. Read Every Line of the Notice

The RFE lists exactly what USCIS wants. It usually separates missing initial evidence from additional evidence needed to establish eligibility. Make a checklist that matches each item.

### 3. Gather Evidence for Each Item

* Official documents such as birth certificates, marriage certificates, tax transcripts, and employer letters
* Translations with a certification of accuracy for any non-English document
* Supporting records such as leases, bills, bank statements, photos, and affidavits, where relevant

### 4. Answer What Was Asked

Do not submit unrelated documents. Extra material can confuse the officer and raise new questions. Provide evidence that directly addresses each stated deficiency.

### 5. Write a Clear Cover Letter

Include an index of exhibits and a short explanation of how each item answers the request. A well-organized response makes the officer's job easier.

### 6. Submit Everything Together

USCIS requires one complete response. If you send documents in several packages, the agency treats it as a request to decide on the record you submitted first. Gather everything before you send it.

### 7. Include the Original RFE Notice

Place the notice on top of your package. USCIS uses it to route your response to the right officer.

### 8. Keep Proof of Delivery

Use a trackable mail method and keep copies of everything you send.

## Common Mistakes That Hurt Cases

* **Missing the deadline.** It is final.
* **Sending a partial response.** Incomplete submissions are judged on what is there.
* **Ignoring part of the notice.** Every listed item must be addressed or explained.
* **Submitting documents in a foreign language without a translation.**
* **Providing inconsistent information.** Dates, names, and addresses should match across documents.
* **Waiting until the last week.** Records from other countries or agencies can take weeks to obtain.
* **Trying to handle a complex RFE on your own.** It's the biggest mistake we run into. Once a response has been submitted, it's usually too late for an attorney to fix it.
* **Sending documents to your attorney piecemeal.** When things drag, it's usually because the client takes a while to reply or sends documents over a few at a time. Every new batch has to be reviewed again. On a short deadline, that time adds up fast.

## What Happens After You Respond

1. USCIS reviews your response and the evidence already on file.
2. The officer may approve, deny, or in some cases issue a second request or a NOID.
3. Processing times vary by form and service center. You can check your case status online with your receipt number.
4. If the case is denied, review the decision for options and deadlines right away.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does an RFE mean my case will be denied?

No. An RFE means USCIS needs more information. Many cases are approved after a complete and well-documented response. Denials most often follow incomplete, late, or contradictory responses.

### Can I ask USCIS for more time?

No. USCIS does not grant extensions. It has also cut RFE deadlines from 84 days to 33 days, which leaves you very little time. Find the deadline printed on your notice and start pulling documents together right away. Your response has to be in before that date.

### What if I cannot get a required document?

If a document does not exist or is unavailable from the issuing authority, USCIS may accept secondary evidence, such as affidavits or alternate records, along with an explanation. An attorney can help you document why the primary record is unavailable. Say you don't have a birth certificate. An affidavit from a family member can be used as secondary evidence, and it's usually accepted.

### Do I need to resubmit documents I already filed?

Generally you should address the specific items requested. Where earlier evidence is relevant, reference it in your cover letter and include a copy when it helps the officer connect the dots.

### How long will the decision take after my response?

Processing times vary by form type and service center. Check the USCIS case status page and processing time tool using your receipt number. You can also submit a case inquiry if your case is outside normal processing time.

### Does an RFE affect my work permit or status?

It can. If your lawful status or work authorization depends on the pending application, delays can affect you. Raise any time-sensitive issues early with your attorney, who can advise on options.

### Can I send my response by email?

Follow the delivery instructions in the notice. Most RFE responses must be mailed to the address listed, and filings made online should be answered through the online account. Always keep proof of submission.
